# Gap-targeted questions — 5.1, after the 10 Sep blurt

Six items, built only from what was missed. Not the lesson's 18-question quiz.

1. `correction` Glia outnumber neurons roughly ten to one. True or false, and what is the
   actual figure?

2. `number` Fill the three quantities: about ___ neurons, each forming ___ to ___ synapses,
   giving roughly ___ connections.

3. `number` Conduction speed on an unmyelinated axon is about ___ m/s; on a myelinated axon
   up to ___ m/s.

4. `definition` State the neuron doctrine, and say what view it replaced.

5. `structure` Name the four glial types and what each one does.

6. `mechanism` Myelination is finished at birth — true or false? If false, when does it
   finish, and which region is last?

---
<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. False — close to 1:1 by direct counting. The 10:1 figure predates about 2010.
2. ~86 billion · 1,000 · 10,000 · ~100 trillion.
3. ~1 m/s · up to 120 m/s.
4. Cajal: neurons are separate cells that communicate across gaps. It replaced the view that
   the nervous system is one continuous network — and it is what makes a synapse, and therefore
   neurotransmission and psychiatric drugs, possible at all.
5. Oligodendrocytes — myelin in brain and spinal cord. Schwann cells — myelin elsewhere.
   Astrocytes — nutrients, chemical environment, blood–brain barrier. Microglia — debris and immunity.
6. False. It continues through childhood and adolescence; the frontal lobes finish in the mid-twenties.

</details>
