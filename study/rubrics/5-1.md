## Definitions
- Neuron — a cell specialised for receiving, processing and transmitting information `definition`
- Neuron doctrine — Cajal: neurons are **separate cells communicating across gaps**, not one continuous network `definition`

## Structure
- Dendrites **receive** `structure`
- Cell body / soma — holds the nucleus, keeps the cell alive, **sums** incoming signals `structure`
- Axon — single fibre, **transmits** away from the cell body `structure`
- Terminal buttons — **release** the chemical messenger `structure`
- Synapse — the junction including the gap `structure`
- Direction: dendrites → cell body → axon → terminal buttons `mechanism`
- Axon length: fraction of a mm inside the brain, up to **~1 metre** spinal cord to foot `number`

## Scale
- ~**86 billion** neurons `number`
- **1,000–10,000** connections each `number`
- ~**100 trillion** connections total `number`

## Three types
- Sensory — receptors → CNS `structure`
- Motor — CNS → muscles and glands `structure`
- Interneuron — neuron to neuron; the processing between input and output; **the large majority** `structure`

## Myelin
- Fatty insulating layer on the axon, **formed from glial cells** `mechanism`
- Nodes of Ranvier — the exposed gaps `definition`
- Saltatory conduction — the signal **jumps** node to node `mechanism`
- Speed: ~**1 m/s** unmyelinated → up to **120 m/s** myelinated `number`
- **Not complete at birth** — continues through childhood and adolescence; frontal lobes finish in the **mid-twenties** `mechanism`

## Glia
- Oligodendrocytes — myelin in **brain and spinal cord** `structure`
- Schwann cells — myelin in **the rest of the body** `structure`
- Astrocytes — nutrients, chemical environment, part of the **blood–brain barrier** `structure`
- Microglia — remove damaged cells and debris; the immune cells `structure`

## Application
- MS: the immune system attacks **myelin**; the neurons are **initially intact**; symptoms come from lost insulation `application`
- Withdrawal reflex: sensory → interneuron **in the cord** → motor; the brain is informed afterwards, which is why the hand moves before pain is felt `application`
- Damage to myelin on *sensory* neurons of a limb → numbness with **movement spared**; motor damage would instead give weakness `application`

## Corrections to standard accounts
- Glia do **not** outnumber neurons 10:1 — direct counting puts it close to **1:1** `correction`
