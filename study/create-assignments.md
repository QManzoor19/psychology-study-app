# Create-level assignments

One per topic, for the eleven topics with written notes. Bloom level 6: you have to
**produce something that did not exist**, not summarise something that does.

Each takes 20–40 minutes and is written, not thought about. Every one has a **trap** —
the shape a weak answer takes — because the failure mode is predictable and usually the
same: describing the material instead of using it.

Mark them against the rubric points. Three of four is a pass; the trap is an automatic fail
however good the rest is.

---

## Topic 01 · What Psychology Is
**Design a "zombie myth" detector.** Write a five-question checklist a non-psychologist could
apply to any claim about people they meet online, that would have caught learning styles,
the Mozart effect and 10%-of-your-brain.

- Each question must be answerable by a non-expert without looking anything up
- At least one question addresses *how the claim would fail* if it were false
- Test it: apply all five to one true finding and one myth, and show it separates them
- **Trap:** questions that only an expert could answer ("was the effect size adequate?")

## Topic 02 · A Short History & the Schools
**Write the manifesto for a seventh school.** Name a school that does not exist, state the
question it asks, the methods it licenses, and the school it is a reaction against.

- The question must be one the existing six answer badly, and you say which and why
- Specify a method — what would its researchers actually *do* on a Tuesday
- State what would count as evidence against your school
- **Trap:** a school that is just "all six combined". Every real school gained power by ruling something out

## Topic 03 · Research Methods
**Design a study, then break it.** Design an experiment to test whether background music
affects revision. Then write the referee report that rejects it.

- IV, DV, operational definitions, and how participants are assigned
- The referee report must find at least one confound, one validity threat and one ethical issue
- Then a v2 that survives your own referee
- **Trap:** a referee report that only asks for a bigger sample

## Topic 04 · Statistics & the Evidence Crisis
**Write a pre-registration for a finding you want to be true.** Pick any claim from Part I,
and pre-register the study honestly enough that you could not p-hack it.

- Hypothesis stated so that a specific result would refute it
- Sample size justified *before* the data, with the effect size you would care about
- Analysis plan fixed in advance, including what you do with outliers
- **Trap:** leaving yourself one "we will also explore…" clause. That clause is the garden of forking paths

## Topic 05 · Neurons & Neural Communication
**Invent a drug and predict its side effects.** Specify a drug by its point of action —
one of the six — and its target transmitter. Then predict what else it does.

- Name the mechanism precisely enough to say agonist or antagonist and why
- Predict three side effects that follow from pathway overlap, not from guesswork
- Say what tolerance to it would look like, and what withdrawal would therefore look like
- **Trap:** a drug with no side effects. A molecule in the bloodstream reaches every matching receptor in the body

## Topic 06 · The Nervous System & the Brain
**Design a lesion.** Invent a patient with damage that would produce one specific deficit
while sparing three closely related abilities — then say which region and why.

- The spared abilities must be ones a naive reader would expect to be lost
- Explain why this pattern is a dissociation and what it would establish
- Name the second patient you would need to make it a *double* dissociation
- **Trap:** a deficit so broad it implicates half the brain

## Topic 07 · Studying the Brain
**Write the press release, then the correction.** Take any real finding from Part II. Write
the overclaiming 150-word press release a university would issue. Then annotate it.

- The release must commit at least three of the six checklist errors, deliberately
- Annotate each with the specific error and what the data actually support
- Rewrite it into a version that is accurate and still worth publishing
- **Trap:** a release so absurd no one would print it. It has to be plausible to be a good specimen

## Topic 08 · Genes, Evolution & Plasticity
**Kill your own adaptive story.** Invent an evolutionary explanation for a modern behaviour.
Then design the study that would show it is wrong.

- The hypothesis must forbid something — state what result would sink it
- Design a test where a cultural explanation predicts the opposite outcome
- Say what the heritability of the trait would and would not tell you about it
- **Trap:** a hypothesis compatible with any result. If you cannot state the disconfirming finding, you have written a just-so story

## Topic 09 · Consciousness & Sleep
**Design a consciousness test for something that cannot speak.** An infant, an octopus, or
a language model. Specify what you would measure and what would convince you.

- State in advance what result would count as evidence *against* consciousness
- Address why absence of report is not absence of experience
- Say honestly which part of your test the hard problem defeats
- **Trap:** a test that measures intelligence or responsiveness instead of awareness

## Topic 10 · Sensation & Psychophysics
**Design a new sense.** Specify a physical energy humans do not detect, and a device that
converts it into one we do.

- Name the transduction step: energy in, neural signal out, and where it enters
- Predict the absolute threshold and what adaptation would do to it within an hour
- Say what a signal detection analysis would show about its false alarms
- **Trap:** a device that displays a number. That is reading a dial, not sensing

## Topic 11 · The Senses
**Build an illusion.** Design a stimulus that would make a person perceive something that
is not there, using a mechanism from this topic.

- Name the mechanism it exploits and the stage of processing it acts on
- Predict who it would fail on, and why
- Say what the illusion demonstrates about normal perception — illusions are only interesting
  when they reveal the rule they break
- **Trap:** describing an existing illusion. Adapt one at minimum, and say what you changed

## Marking your own

Ask three questions of anything you produce:

1. **Could this have been written from the summary alone?** If yes, it is level 2, not level 6.
2. **Does it forbid anything?** A design that accommodates every outcome is decoration.
3. **Did you find your own error?** Every one of these has a self-refutation step, because
   spotting the flaw in your own design is the skill being trained.
